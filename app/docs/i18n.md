# Languages

The app speaks English and French. English is the source: the text is
written in English in the templates and scripts, and each other language
has a catalog keyed by that English text (`internal/i18n/fr/*.json`).
A string missing from a catalog shows in English.

## Which language a page is in

1. The member's own choice (Profile → Language: Automatic, English,
   Français; `people.locale`).
2. Else the language of the community or band the page belongs to (its
   admin page or settings; `communities.locale`, `bands.locale`): its
   pages, its songbooks, a song opened from one of them.
3. Else the browser's (Accept-Language).
4. Else English.

`internal/httpapi/i18n.go` decides it per request; `render` picks the
pages parsed in that language (`webassets.TemplatesFor`).

## Writing a translatable page

In templates:

- `{{t "Add a song"}}` for a text; with values, a fmt format:
  `{{t "%s’s songs" .Name}}`, `{{t "%d of %d done" .Done .Of}}`.
- Plurals: `{{tn .N "%d song" "%d songs"}}` (French says 0 and 1 in the
  singular). Replace `{{.N}} song{{if ne .N 1}}s{{end}}` with it.
- Dates: `{{date .StartsAt "Monday 2 January"}}` in place of
  `{{.StartsAt.Format "Monday 2 January"}}` (day and month names in the
  page's language; a `*time.Time` works, nil gives ""). Put the day before
  the month in layouts ("2 January", not "January 2").
- Attributes too: `placeholder`, `title`, `aria-label`, `alt`, a
  `confirm()` text.
- A sentence with a link in it: translate the pieces around the link, or
  make the link's text its own string; never put HTML in a catalog.
- `{{lang}}` is the page's language: `<html lang="{{lang}}">`.
- A word that means two things gets a context before a bar, one word, no
  space: `{{t "notation|Staff"}}` (a staff, *portée*), `{{t
  "people|Staff"}}` (a team, *équipe*); English shows what follows the
  bar. Same in scripts: `t('notation|Staff')`. Use it for short, generic
  words (Staff, Key, Tab, Off, Done, Start, Track…) and for any fragment
  of a split sentence ("The", "tab."): `song-page|The`.
- Text computed in Go and printed by a template (a label, a kind):
  translate it where it's printed, `{{t .Label}}`, and put its values in
  the catalog.

In scripts (every page loads `/i18n/{lang}.js` from the `pwa-head`
partial; a page without it must load it before its scripts):

- `t('Saved')`, `t('%d songs selected', n)` (`%d` and `%s`, in order),
  `tn(n, '%d song', '%d songs')`.
- Their strings go in the `js-*.json` catalogs: only those reach the
  browser.

Never translate: song titles, artists, lyrics, chord symbols, people's and
groups' names, anything a person typed, URLs and paths, CSS classes and
ids, `data-*` values, an option's `value=` (what the server reads),
keyboard keys, file formats (MP3, PDF, Guitar Pro), the app's name
(Songbooks).

## French

- **Vous**, everywhere.
- Typography: ’ for the apostrophe (as the English uses); a no-break space
  (U+00A0) before `:` and inside « »; a narrow no-break space (U+202F)
  before `;` `!` `?`. Sentence case for titles and buttons, as in English.
- Short, plain, the same tone as the English (which is terse and
  concrete). Buttons are verbs: *Ajouter*, *Enregistrer*.
- Note names: chord symbols stay as written (C, Am7, F♯m: French pop,
  rock and jazz musicians read them). A singer's notes (voice ranges, the
  tuner's notes in French) are do ré mi fa sol la si, with the French
  octave numbers: middle C (C4) is **do3** (`internal/i18n.NoteName`).
