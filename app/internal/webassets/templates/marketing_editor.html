No import/export round trip, no "upload a PDF and hope it stays in sync." What you type on the left is exactly what renders on the right — on screen, in the PDF, on a music stand.

Not a mockup — the actual in-app editor

The bar-by-bar chord builder from the real app: pick a root, a quality, how long it holds, and the chart above updates as you go. No markup to memorize unless you want to drop into the Source tab directly.

And when you need paper

The same file drives the PDF export, native to the app — no separate layout pass, nothing to keep in sync by hand.

Header fields

Title, artist, key, tempo, time signature — plain key: value lines, nothing to configure in a sidebar first.

Grid or lyrics, same chart

A chords-only section renders as a staff; a [Chord]word line puts the chord right above the word it changes on — not glued to every syllable.

Notes stay with the song

An arrangement note — "let it ring," "capo 2" — is one line in the file and shows up right where it's relevant, on screen and in the PDF.

Already have the chords somewhere else?

Paste a link, not a blank page. The import wizard reads the chord grid out of it and drops it straight into the editor as a draft — you fix what it got wrong, you don't start from zero.

From Ultimate Guitar

Paste a tab page URL — the chord-over-lyric text comes in pre-formatted, ready to clean up.

From iReal Pro

Bring in a song or a whole playlist from an iReal Pro forum link — chords, structure, and key arrive already split into sections.

Always a draft first

Nothing imports straight into your songbook unedited — it lands in the editor so you can fix the inevitable wrong chord before saving.

Why a text format, not a drag-and-drop builder

A chart is a document, and documents are text — that gets you three things a purely visual editor can't.

One source, every output

The same file renders the web chart, the printable PDF, and the setlist export — there's nothing to keep in sync by hand.

Fast to type, fast to fix

Transposing a section, fixing a wrong chord, adding a verse — it's a text edit, not a sequence of clicks through a toolbar.

Still has a real editor

You don't have to hand-write the grammar — the in-app editor gives you section chips, a bar-by-bar chord builder, and live preview on top of the same format.
